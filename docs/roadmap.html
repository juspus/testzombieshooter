Why it plays like a flash game

Look

It looks flat

Every surface is a flat colour on a box, nothing goes through post-processing, and the camera is bolted in place: no head bob, no sway, no kick. The pixel-art zombies are the most stylised thing in the game, and the world around them doesn't match.

Fixed in Wave 2

Feel

Hits don't land

A shot that connects gives almost nothing back. No hit marker, the zombie doesn't flinch, no blood leaves the body, and a kill is a 0.75 s tip-over before the body vanishes. Zombie footsteps and plank hits play flat in both ears, so you can't tell which window is under attack.

Fixed in Wave 1

Loop

Every run is the same run

The buy order is solved (pistol, shotgun, AK, Deagle), the whole cabin is open from the first second, and between runs only cosmetics carry over. Nothing gives you a reason to play tomorrow that you didn't have today.

Fixed in Waves 3 and 4

The order, and why

Feel first. It touches every second of play and it's the cheapest work on this list. Retention systems on top of a game that doesn't feel good keep nobody, because the first session decides whether there is a second one.

Then the look, so the game reads as deliberate in screenshots, in a portal thumbnail and in the first ten seconds. Then the run and the long-term hooks, which are what actually bring people back.

What we're not doing

  • Realistic models and texturesMonths of asset work, heavier downloads, slower phones, and it still loses side by side with games made in real engines.
  • Real money for random rewardsThe Mystery Box costs in-game money only. No loot boxes, nothing that buys power.
  • Fear-of-missing-out pressureNothing you pay for expires. Seasonal items are earned by playing.
  • Energy or lives systemsNever stop someone who wants to play another run.
  • Crafting, base building, open worldComplexity that dilutes the tight defend-the-cabin loop.
  • 4-player before solo is stickyMost runs are solo. Fix the invite friction first.
