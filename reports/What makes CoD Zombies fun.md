# Why Round-Based Zombies Hooks Players, and Where It Breaks

Call of Duty Zombies is fun because a **points economy turns a simple survive-the-next-round loop into a stream of spend-or-save decisions**, played with friends, on tight atmospheric maps, with optional secrets and public mastery goals layered on top. It breaks when the loop is abandoned (BO2 TranZit, Vanguard, MW3), when power is sold or randomised (GobbleGums, Elixirs), when launch content is thin, and when runs or progress are lost to disconnects, crashes and grind. The evidence is stronger on what went wrong by era than on what feels good moment to moment: the researchers could not open any web page, so everything rests on search-result summaries, fan wikis and forum snippets, with almost no verbatim player quotes and no Reddit text. Our owner's complaint that "progression feels flat" maps onto the best-supported lessons: roguelites that guarantee every run earns something, unlock options rather than raw power, and give each run a secondary goal (Risk of Rain 2, Vampire Survivors), plus a daily seeded run with a spoiler-free share (Spelunky, Wordle). The top priorities for our game are a run-end debrief that always shows progress, feat-based unlocks that add variety not power, a short first-run ramp, a daily run built to be shared, and protection against lost runs. We should also keep skill-paying money rules, give specials audible tells, add a bounded luck layer to the weapon box, and offer a difficulty or mutator ladder for veterans. We must not copy any CoD names or items, sell power, or ship half-built systems.

## The loop turns money into decisions, and skill into money

The sourced core is an economy where **skill pays and every purchase competes with another**. In the Black Ops 2 era a body hit paid 10 points, a normal kill 60, a headshot kill 100 and a knife kill about 130, and each rebuilt board paid 10 ([GamerGuides BO2](https://www.gamerguides.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-ii/guide/zombies-guide/zombies-mode/overview); [Millenium BO2 guide](https://www.millenium.org/guide/99455.html)). Prices then pull against each other: solo Quick Revive at 500 against Juggernog at 2,500 ([Black Ops In Depth](https://blackopsindepth.neocities.org/perk-a-colas.html)), and later Pack-a-Punch tiers as continuing sinks (5,000/15,000/30,000 Essence in Cold War, per [Blizzard News tips](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23699738/gear-up-for-mauer-der-toten-top-10-tips-for-black-ops-cold-war-zombies)). Our inference is that the knife-pays-most spread rewards closeness and risk, so when a PC Gamer piece quotes a BO6 player saying the points system no longer rewards prioritising headshots versus melee versus body kills ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/call-of-duty/call-of-duty-black-ops-6-zombies-players-have-found-an-easter-egg-thatll-give-you-2-000-free-points-but-you-have-to-be-good-at-trick-shots)), a core lever is reportedly gone (the official BO6 table was not found).

Escalation forces the spending. A community Black Ops guide puts base zombie health at 150, rising 100 per round to 950 at round 9 ([Spookystorm's guide](https://randomonialand.fandom.com/wiki/Spookystorm%27s_Guide_to_Black_Ops_Zombies)), and fans describe a gradual ramp with no time limit ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/call-of-duty-game/why-round-based-zombies-maps-popular-call-duty)). Exact formulas are disputed (a spawn-delay multiplier and a count cap at round 30 appear in forum and wiki sources that conflict: [CoDZombies forum](https://callofdutyzombies.com/topic/137395-zombie-charts), [Project Lazarus](https://project-lazarus.fandom.com/wiki/Rounds)). Our inference is that starting guns stop being viable, so the economy must feed upgrades. Fan wikis describe metered randomness around that economy. The weapon box moves after a "bear" outcome that never appears in the first three uses, carries 15% chance on uses four to seven, and is certain after eight at the starting location ([CoD wiki Mystery Box](https://callofduty.fandom.com/wiki/Mystery_Box)). Power-up drops are described as a 2% roll per kill, capped at 4 per round with a team-points requirement ([Project Lazarus Power-Ups](https://project-lazarus.fandom.com/wiki/Power-Ups)), and special rounds always end with a Max Ammo drop ([CoD wiki Max Ammo](https://breezewiki.discard.no/callofduty/wiki/Max_Ammo)). These wiki numbers are unverified against official code, but the design reading is ours and plausible: both are loss-limited gambling, not pure chance. Barricade repair paying 10 per board and the Carpenter power-up fixing windows tie the safest activity to income ([Project Lazarus strategies](https://project-lazarus.fandom.com/wiki/Community_Power-Up_Strategies)). Door gating converts money into map access; the door, wall-weapon and trap costs were not retrieved.

The mode began almost by accident: a developer recalls building it in spare time during World at War, with no voice acting "because there was no budget" ([GameSpot oral history, via search summary](https://gamespot.com/articles/call-of-duty-zombies-an-oral-history-of-the-unlike/1100-6442252/)), and a Treyarch blog post called it "pretty much an accident" ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/the-secret-history-behind-call-of-dutys-zombie-mode.220986)). Game Informer disputes who was on the mode then ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/feature/the-convoluted-history-of-call-of-dutys-bloody-zombies-mode)). Later designers state the aims: Jason Blundell says the team designs so players find new things after many playthroughs ([GameZone](https://gamezone.com/?p=111614)) and assumes players are "intelligent and curious" ([Activision blog](https://blog.activision.com/call-of-duty/archives/treyarch-zombies-director-jason-blundell-on-call-of-duty-black-ops-4-zombies)); Kevin Drew frames design around cooperation ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-call-of-duty-black-ops-4s-zombies-mode-is-social-deep-and-customizable/)) and says round-based "is very much about that feeling of claustrophobia" ([GamesBeat BO7](https://gamesbeat.com/how-treyarch-designed-black-ops-7s-zombies-interview/)).

## Format departures and paid boosts sank eras; tight round-based maps won them

Reception tracks a few repeatable causes, mostly opinion from forums and secondary outlets. Black Ops 1 is the era most often named as fans' favourite: a Reddit thread passed 13,000 upvotes with most players naming it, and Kino der Toten is praised for atmosphere and simple layout, though part of its popularity is attributed to being the default map ([GGRecon](https://ggrecon.com/articles/black-ops-1-zombies-lauded-as-one-of-the-best-multiplayer-eras-ever), via search summary). A reader-vote tally of about 21% for Kino is of unverified method and indicative only. In Black Ops 2, **TranZit is the clearest cautionary tale**: fan and guide sources say the bus, fog and hostile Denizens are why the community "doesn't like it that much" ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=897802526)), and one reviewer called most of its buildables "straight up useless" ([Backloggd](https://www.backloggd.com/u/Vgkid2049/review/895707)). Our reading is that complaints cluster on friction between objectives and the survive-in-a-room loop, while Buried's praised pieces (a giant who breaks barriers, a time bomb) are interactive tools ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/black-ops-2-buried-arthur-leroy-how-to-free-all-booze-candy-bo2/)).

Paid or per-player power is the second flashpoint. Black Ops 3 let Call of Duty Points buy vials that became one-use GobbleGums, a change some saw as the first monetisation that affected core play ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-3-didnt-have-microtransactions-now-it-does); the "first to affect core gameplay" claim is from a weak student essay). Black Ops 4's Elixirs drew a split verdict: "the most in-depth, customizable Zombies version yet" ([ComicBook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/2018/10/19/call-of-duty-black-ops-4-zombies-review)) versus a reviewer asking whether co-op works when Elixirs pit players against each other ([Stuff](https://www.stuff.tv/?p=349565)), and removing Juggernog was "one of the most controversial changes" ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-4-zombies-perks-ranked/)). Even in Black Ops 6 a repeat-loot GobbleGum complaint passed 1,000 upvotes ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/call-of-duty/black-ops-6-players-slam-annoying-gobblegum-for-repeat-loot-3058469/)).

Format and content come third. Vanguard was labelled "the worst iteration of Call of Duty Zombies we have ever seen" ([GamePur](https://gamepur.com/?p=252671)) for thin launch content and a non-round-based first map ([Charlie Intel](https://www.charlieintel.com/call-of-duty-vanguard/cod-vanguard-zombies-review-empty-experience-sees-series-take-giant-step-back-146825/)); MW3's open-world extraction format left fans saying the mode was "missing everything that made it special" ([Charlie Intel](https://charlieintel.com/call-of-duty/modern-warfare-3-fans-claim-zombies-mode-is-missing-everything-that-made-it-special-278307)), though some critics said it grew on them ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/modern-warfare-3-zombies-revolutionises-a-legacy-of-undead-fps)). Black Ops 6 restored round-based play and was welcomed, with Terminus praised for darkness and claustrophobia ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/call-of-duty-game/black-ops-6-zombies-review)); Black Ops 7's Zombies was called the package's "strongest pillar" but safe, "doesn't take many risks" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-7/review)). Cold War was praised for serving hardcore and casual players ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-cold-war-the-kotaku-review-1845692625)) while one critic argued it was "too easy" by round 7 ([Lv1 Gaming](https://lv1gaming.com/call-of-duty-cold-war-zombies-is-too-easy-and-thats-a-problem/)); a Steam poster says late-round armoured zombies are too tanky. These coexist because the complaints are about different phases of the power curve. The pattern, which is our inference from several eras, is that leaving the round-based loop drew the strongest backlash, and a "safe" return was forgiven.

## Players stay for friends, mastery and secrets, and leave over lost runs and grind

The strongest stated draw in the sampled material is **playing with friends**, with discovery second. Forum commenters credit the mode with friendships and parties of friends ([callofdutyzombies.com](https://www.callofdutyzombies.com/topic/185729-what-keeps-you-loving-zombies)), a reviewer describes reuniting with old squadmates ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/call-of-duty/how-did-i-not-know-that-call-of-duty-modern-warfare-3-zombies-was-this-much-fun)), and BO3 Steam reviews cite playing with friends as the reason to buy (paraphrased; anecdotal). One fragment recalls "all the easter egg hunting" as "a crazy amount of fun" (quote via search summary, thread unconfirmed). The sample is small and biased toward press and forums. Self-determination research offers a frame: violent content adds little once competence, autonomy and relatedness are met ([Przybylski, Ryan and Rigby 2009](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2009_PrzbylskiRyanRigby_PSPB-1.pdf)); mapping round records to competence, co-op to relatedness and routes to autonomy is our own untested application.

Mastery is public and endurance-based: a first legitimate round 100 on Call of the Dead took a streamer about 61 hours in April 2024 ([GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/cod-zombies-player-breaks-stunning-call-of-the-dead-record-13-years-later)), and a BO3 record was beaten after nearly 300 hours ([Charlie Intel](https://www.charlieintel.com/zombies-world-record-beaten-after-nearly-300-hours-of-call-of-duty-black-ops-3/116828/)). Secrets are a communal sport: a 2021 Cold War Discord of nearly 1,000 members existed to find egg partners ([r/ColdWarZombies mirror](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/ColdWarZombies)), and a BO4 egg was found four years after release ([GGRecon](https://cms.ggrecon.com/articles/an-incredible-black-ops-4-zombies-easter-egg-has-been-found-four-years-later)). Obscurity has a price: Treyarch reportedly simplified Cold War eggs because under two percent of players saw them ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/cod-black-ops-cold-war-zombies-easter-eggs-easier-secrets-ps4-xbox-pc)), Blundell admits making the BO3 egg deliberately convoluted ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/cod-blops-3-call-of-duty-black-ops-impossible-easter-egg-2000683922)), and BO3 commenters report quitting from tedium ("WAY too many tasks to do that arent even fun") and a step needing four players (fragments via search summary). Newcomers face a wall that Treyarch answered with a BO4 tutorial and BO6 Guided/Directed modes, where Drew says rounds are capped and it "only really gets harder than round 15" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-treyarch-designed-black-ops-7s-zombies-interview/)); one guide advises first-timers to play solo to learn ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/960189-call-of-duty-black-ops/57205128)).

Quitting is driven at least as much by **lost runs and lost progress** as by difficulty, though the evidence is a handful of Steam anecdotes. Cold War posters complain that one player's rage-quit ends everyone's match ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1985810/discussions/0/4629233576852851989)); MW3 posters say rounds do not count when a teammate leaves and that "the only reason to play zombies is to grind guns and levels" ([Steam MW3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1938090/discussions/0/4631482569784864289)); record runs end in crashes ([Comicbook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/call-of-duty-black-ops-cold-war-noahj456-zombies-crash)); and a Cold War connection bug left one player saying they would not buy another CoD ([Charlie Intel](https://www.charlieintel.com/black-ops-cold-war/cold-war-zombies-players-furious-as-game-breaking-connection-interrupted-issues-continue-120894/)). The most-cited BO6 grievance was no co-op pause, with a pause timer announced for a later season ([Wikipedia BO6 Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Black_Ops_6_Zombies); [FandomWire via IMDb](https://m.imdb.com/news/ni65072737)). Grind-only motivation collapses when progression changes; run goals should be the core.

## Peers solve retention with guaranteed progress, options over power, and a paced director

Comparable games address the owner's exact complaint. Vampire Survivors' meta loop means a run never restarts from zero, and commentary criticises games where the first upgrade takes many winning runs ([Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/success-vampire-survivors)). A design write-up separates "permanent power" from "variety not power" unlocks ([saschb2b](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/concepts/meta-as-variety-not-power)); a critic argues Dead Cells unlocks matter because they open new ways to play, and that faster restarts raise "one more run" ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/opinion/hades-endgame-content-does-what-dead-cells-never-could/)). Risk of Rain 2 ties unlocks to challenges (a character after five Teleporter completions) with no permanent stat meta ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/risk-of-rain-2-character-unlock/)), the closest sourced model for the planned feat unlocks. Counter-view, from a low-authority thread: strong meta makes losing feel like "should have grinded more". Failures are instructive: Back 4 Blood's card system was found unintuitive and granular, with a separate solo deck ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/back-4-bloods-solo-mode-may-get-a-progression-overhaul-3066363)); Darktide launched with one of four crafting aspects and RNG-heavy loot ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/darktides-crafting-system-isnt-quite-ready-because-fatshark-ripped-it-out-and-started-over-mid-development)); Deep Rock Galactic earned goodwill by remaking criticised paid skins ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/deep-rock-galactic-studio-remakes-cosmetic-dlc-after-fans-complain-everyone-gets-the-original-designs-for-free)).

Pacing and structure come from Left 4 Dead: Valve's slides list "Generate Dramatic Game Pacing" as an explicit goal ([Valve PDF, snippet only](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)), special infected carry distinct spawn cues ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/great-moments-in-pc-gaming-dont-startle-the-witch)), and World War Z's swarm was judged short of that tension ([God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/world-war-z-review/)). Killing Floor 2 chooses its last-wave boss randomly ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Floor_2)). For repeat play, Spelunky's daily is one seed, one attempt, ranked by money and level, with the weakness that boards reward greed ([Spelunky wiki](https://spelunky.fandom.com/wiki/Daily_Challenge_Mode_(HD)); [The Ludite](https://theludite.com/2014/06/23/spelunky-when-scoring-goes-bad/)); Wordle's no-spoiler share grid and one puzzle a day drove growth ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success)); Slay the Spire's daily is reportedly computed locally from one integer ([Matt Warden](https://mattwarden.com/projects/spire-daily/), hobbyist analysis). Evolve's paywalled roles and queue death spiral argue against depending on matchmaking ([Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/why-did-evolve-fail/)). On feel, Vlambeer-style layered feedback works, but a guide warns one shake for every event stops conveying information ([game juice guide](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/articles/screen-shake-guide), blog).

## Prioritised action points for our game

These are original mechanics derived from the findings. They assume what the brief states about our game (I did not audit the code, so check each against `docs/systems.md` and the Daily and Director seeds). P0 means start now, P1 next, P2 later. Effort: S under a few days, M about a week or two, L a multi-week system. Effect statements are expectations, not measured results.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Run-end debrief that always pays out**: every run, including a wave-2 death, grants XP/challenge progress, shows what was earned, and shows distance to the next unlock and a "next best goal" (for example, 2 waves to unlock a loadout). One-tap restart. | Vampire Survivors never restarting from zero (Game Wisdom); faster restart raises "one more run" (Twinfinite); quits driven by lost progress (Steam anecdotes) | Directly answers "no point"; lifts return-after-death; tolerable failure | S | P0 |
| 2 | **Feat unlocks that add options, not power**: "do X in a run" unlocks new starting loadouts, new weapon-box pool entries, new perk choices, new wave modifiers and cosmetics. Show every locked feat from the start. Same unlocked state or a neutral state for Daily boards. | Risk of Rain 2 challenge unlocks; "variety not power" (saschb2b); Dead Cells (Twinfinite); grind-only motivation collapses (Steam MW3) | Gives each run a secondary goal and a reason to try other builds; no stat treadmill; fair leaderboards | M | P0 |
| 3 | **First-run ramp**: wave 1-3 is guided solo-feeling play with one-line hints per new layer (doors, box, bench, perks), a first meaningful beat inside about 5 minutes (door opened, box rolled), and an optional capped "learn" run. | BO4 tutorial, BO6 Guided/Directed, Drew's "only gets harder than round 15"; solo-first advice (GameFAQs); Back 4 Blood complexity wall; short-session advice (DEV, GitNation) | Fewer day-one exits; matches our hint rule | S to M | P0 |

Mutator of the day via `keyedRandom`. | Spelunky daily and its greed critique; Wordle share; Slay the Spire daily computed from a date integer; Deep Rock mutators | A reason to return every day, free distribution, a topic for friends | S to M | P0 |
| 5 | **Run safety**: autosave or resume on tab close, and when a co-op partner drops the survivor continues (bot or solo takeover) instead of the run ending; Versus and Mail get a bot fallback for an empty pool. | Lost runs to disconnects and crashes (Steam anecdotes, Comicbook); BO6 pause request; Evolve queue death spiral | Removes the most-cited rage point; protects investment | M | P0 |

Show the bonus in the money pop. | 10/60/100/130 spread (GamerGuides, Millenium); BO6 "flattened" complaint (PC Gamer, one player) | Skill feels rewarded; cheap to do | S | P1 |

Keep wave-end shop as the valley. | L4D Director pacing (Valve slides, GamesRadar); WWZ swarm without tension | Waves feel authored, not just bigger | M | P1 |
| 9 | **Specials audit**: each special gets a distinct sound cue, a distinct counter and a forced target or movement change; pick the boss variant per run from the seed. Keep specials as variations on the shambling horde. | L4D cues (PC Gamer); Killing Floor 2 random boss; "zombies, not monsters" commenter (other games, low confidence) | Fairness, role play in co-op, variety per run | M | P1 |
| 10 | **Difficulty and mutator ladder for veterans**: opt-in modifiers that raise reward (for example a tougher wave curve, no wave-end shop restock) with a badge, plus an adaptive lighter option. | "Too easy by round 7" vs "too tanky late" (Lv1 Gaming, Steam); flow theory (Game Developer); Drew's capped Directed rounds | Keeps experts and beginners in flow; endless replay value | M | P1 |
| 11 | **Secret hunts with tiers**: one discoverable multi-step secret per map with a visible first step, a solo-completable path, a hint tier and a persistent reward (cosmetic or title). No step requires a second player. | Egg culture (GGRecon, Discord); Blundell on discoverability; tedium and 4-player gating complaints (Steam BO3); under-2% saw eggs (Gfinity) | A discovery and community layer, social proof, long-tail goals | M to L | P2 |
| 12 | **Co-op glue**: a one-link invite, a drop-in, a shared "saved you" revive moment in the debrief, and a joint end-of-run goal. | Friends as the top motive (callofdutyzombies.com, Windows Central); Drew on cooperation | Turns the strongest motive into acquisition | S to M | P1 |

| 14 | **Scaled feel**: hit stop, shake and sound scaled by event size (kill small, headshot, special, boss, downed) with an accessibility toggle; a signature wave-start and wave-clear audio cue. Mind the repo rules (fxBus, no per-frame allocation, delta clamp). | Screenshake guidance (Vlambeer-style guides, blogs); CoD's eerie round-change audio (search summary only, low confidence) | Stronger moment-to-moment feel and recall | S | P2 |

Items 1, 2 and 4 together are the answer to "flat progression": a run always earns something, the earnings open new ways to play, and a daily gives a dated reason to come back. They should ship before anything in the P2 rows.

### What to avoid, based on what players disliked

Do not sell power or boosts, per-player or otherwise: GobbleGums and Elixirs are the recurring flashpoint, and Deep Rock Galactic's cosmetic-only goodwill is the counter-model, so any shop money should buy only looks. Do not replace the wave loop with objectives or extraction: TranZit, Vanguard's first map and MW3 are the most disliked formats; side goals should sit on top of waves. Do not ship a system half-built or launch thin (Vanguard, Darktide); keep each map and mode complete enough to stand alone. Do not gate fun behind obscurity or other players: secrets must be hintable, solo-completable and tedium-free. Do not tie a run's survival to another player's connection. Do not let one run-defining stat or loadout swing survivability for one player in co-op, as the Elixir debate suggests. Do not stack hard-to-read mechanics (Back 4 Blood cards), and do not add unbounded randomness or repeat-loot streaks to the box. Do not make Versus depend on queues (Evolve). Finally, do not copy any CoD names, items or art: express each lesson as our own mechanic (for example the jam-and-relocate box and our own perk and unlock names).

## Confidence and source limits

Confidence is **moderate** for era-level reception patterns (several independent outlets and forums agree on TranZit, Vanguard, MW3 and paid-boost backlash), **moderate** for the roguelite and Daily lessons (secondary analysis, no developer primary sources), and **low** for any numbers. The box, drop-rate and health figures are fan-wiki or forum figures that conflict in places or are unverified against official code, and the 21% map poll and the BO4 health change (150 to 200, [HubPages](https://discover.hubpages.com/games-hobbies/Treyarch-Updates-Call-of-Duty-Black-Ops-4-Zombies-and-Perk-System-Explained), weak) should not be relied on. Player-voice evidence is thin: the quoted fragments are phrases surfaced by search summaries, and no Reddit thread was read. Not found at all: reception of Grief, the Vanguard Shi No Numa remake, Augments as a design and BO6 later maps; box and perk player feelings; flow or "one more round" studies for horde modes; and anything on 2026 titles. Our application of every finding to our game is inference, and each action point should be validated against our own telemetry or a playtest.

## Conclusion

The most useful reading of the research is that Zombies' fun is **a stack, not a mechanic**: a legible ramp, money that makes you choose, friends to share it with, and a layer of secrets and records for the committed. Its failures came from damaging the stack, by leaving the loop, selling or randomising power, or losing the player's run. That reframes the owner's feedback: our shop, box, bench and specials already supply the in-run stack, so the gap is the layer around the run (guaranteed earnings, options-not-power unlocks, a daily ritual, shareable records) and protecting the run itself.

The highest-leverage move is therefore not another weapon or special but making every run leave something behind and giving the player a reason to open the game tomorrow, while holding the line on what players punished: paid power, grind-only goals, run loss and obscurity. The evidence base is thin on first-hand player voices, so the early wins (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) should be shipped small and measured by return rate and run length before the larger bets (secret hunts, difficulty ladder) are built.
